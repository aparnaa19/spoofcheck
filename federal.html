Do federal agencies block spoofed email?

In 2017 DHS issued Binding Operational Directive 18-01. By October 16, 2018, every federal executive branch second-level domain had to publish a valid SPF record and a DMARC policy of p=reject, with DMARC aggregate reports going to DHS (reports@dmarc.cyber.dhs.gov). This page checks all federal executive .gov domains against those rules, live, from your browser.

About 2,300 DNS lookups. Takes a minute or two.

What this does and doesn't measure

Only the parts of BOD 18-01 that are visible in DNS are checked: one valid SPF record, DMARC p=reject applied to 100% of mail, and DHS listed in rua. The directive also covers STARTTLS on mail servers and HTTPS on websites, which this page doesn't test.

The domain list comes from CISA's dotgov-data repository (). Some of these domains are parked or redirect-only, but the directive applies to them too: an unused domain without DMARC is exactly what a phisher wants.

A "lookup failed" result means both DNS resolvers errored for that domain. Running the scan again usually clears it.
